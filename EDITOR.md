# The website editor

There is a page on the website where you write a post, drop in a photo, see
exactly how it will look, and put it up. It is at:

```
hulseyforhouse.com/admin
```

Bookmark it. That address is not linked from anywhere on the site and search
engines are told to ignore it, so nobody finds it by accident.

---

## Signing in

You need to sign in once on each computer or phone you use. Follow whatever the
page shows you — either a **Sign in with GitHub** button, or a short set of
steps to fetch an access key and paste it in. Either way it is a one-time thing;
after that the editor remembers you.

**Before signing in you can still look around.** Every post can be opened and
previewed signed out. The editor only asks who you are at the moment you try to
publish something.

---

## Writing a post

Press **Write a post**. There are five things to fill in, and only three of them
matter much.

**Headline.** What people see on the news card, at the top of the post, and in
Google. Eight to twelve words is the sweet spot.

**One-sentence summary.** This does more work than it looks like it does. It is
the line under the headline on the news page, the preview Facebook and text
messages show, and the description Google puts under your link. Write a complete
sentence. The counter turns green when the length is right.

**Photo.** Drag one in, or click the box and pick one. Landscape photos work
best. Big files are fine — a photo straight off a phone gets resized for you on
the way in, so the website stays fast.

**What is happening in the photo.** Shows as the caption under the picture, and
is what a blind visitor and Google are told the picture shows.

**The post.** Write normally. A blank line starts a new paragraph. The buttons
above the box add a heading, a bullet list, bold, a quote or a link — you never
have to type anything that looks like code.

Then press **Publish**. The post is live in about a minute. The editor tells you
when it is up and gives you the link.

---

## The preview

The right-hand side shows what you are making, updating as you type. Three tabs:

**The page** — the actual post page. Not an impression of it: the editor builds
it with the same template the website uses, so this is the page, a minute early.
The phone and computer buttons at the top right switch screen size.

**News card** — how it will look on the news page and the home page.

**When shared** — what Facebook, LinkedIn and a text message show when someone
shares the link, and what the Google result looks like. This is the one worth
glancing at before publishing. A post with no photo falls back to the campaign
logo here, which is the fastest way to see why a photo is worth adding.

---

## The checks

Under the post there is a short list of checks — headline length, summary
length, whether there is a photo and a caption. They are advice, not rules;
nothing stops you publishing. They are there because those four things are most
of what decides whether a post gets read.

---

## Fixing, hiding and deleting

**To fix a post that is already up,** open it from the list, change what you
need and press Publish. The post keeps the same web address, so any link already
shared, posted or emailed keeps working. This is the reason to edit rather than
delete and repost.

**To take a post down for a while,** press **Hide from site**. It stays saved and
disappears from the website. Open it later and press Publish to bring it back.

**To write now and publish later,** press **Save as hidden**. Same thing: saved,
but not on the website.

**To delete a post for good,** open it and use Delete at the bottom. It comes off
the news page, the feed and the sitemap within a minute or two, and Google drops
it over the following few days.

---

## Things worth knowing

**You cannot break anything.** Every version of every post is kept. Anything can
be undone by whoever looks after the site.

**Nothing goes live until you press Publish.** Not while you are typing, not if
you close the tab.

**Your typing is kept as you go.** If your laptop dies or you close the tab by
mistake, the editor has your unsaved work when you come back.

**Two people editing the same post.** If someone else changed a post while you
had it open, the editor says so before it publishes and lets you decide.

**Search engines look after themselves.** Every time you publish, the page title,
the description, the Facebook and Google previews, the news feed and the sitemap
all update on their own. The only part that matters is the part only you can do:
a clear headline, a real sentence in the summary box, and a photo.

## If something goes wrong

The editor says what happened in plain words at the bottom of the screen. The
two you might see:

**"Could not reach GitHub."** The internet connection dropped. Your work is
safe — press Publish again when you are back online.

**"Saved, but the site did not rebuild."** The post is saved; the step that turns
it into a page did not finish. Pass that message to whoever looks after the site.

Everything on the News page can still be posted from GitHub's Issues tab
instead — see **POSTING.md**. Both ways write the same thing, so you can use
whichever you prefer.
