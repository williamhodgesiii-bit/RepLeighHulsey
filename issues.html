Six things. No filler.

This is what District 15 brings up at the gym, at church, and in the checkout line — and what Leigh is doing about each one in Montgomery.

01

Education

Classrooms That Work

Teachers had been saying the same thing for years: the phones are winning. Leigh sponsored HB166 — the FOCUS Act, for Freeing Our Classrooms of Unnecessary Screens for Safety — and made it law.

Under the FOCUS Act, students in Alabama public elementary and secondary schools can't possess or use a wireless device during the school day unless it's powered off and stored away. Bell to bell. It carves out emergencies, students with an Individualized Education Plan or a Section 504 plan, and genuine instructional use.

The bill also requires local boards of education to adopt an internet safety policy for school devices, and requires students to complete a social media safety course before they begin eighth grade.

It passed the House 79–15 and the Senate 30–2, and Governor Ivey signed it in May 2025. In a building where almost nothing is unanimous, those numbers say something.

02

Economy

Jobs and Small Business

Leigh has made payroll. She owns CrossFit Alabaster and served as president of the Alabaster Business Alliance. That's a different starting point than most people who vote on tax policy.

In 2026 she carried HB399, which limits the maximum tax abatement available to data processing centers to 20 years — down from 30 — unless a project meets community investment thresholds, beginning January 1, 2027. It also restricts the sales tax abatement on the state's 4% sales and use tax for facilities drawing 100 megawatts or more.

Alabama still competes hard for those projects and the capital investment they bring. The change is that the state stops signing deals that outlast the officials who approved them, the schools that absorb the growth, and often the technology itself. The Senate passed it unanimously.

03

Public Safety

Backing the People Who Run Toward It

District 15 is one of the fastest-growing parts of Alabama. Population arrives faster than public safety budgets do, and the gap shows up as response times.

Leigh has supported legislation strengthening public safety, and works directly with local departments on equipment needs and grant applications — the practical side of the job that never makes a headline but does put gear on a truck.

If you run a department in District 15 and there's a funding source you think you might qualify for, that's exactly the kind of call this office wants.

04

Infrastructure

Roads and Growth

Everybody wants the growth. Nobody wants the traffic. Managing the distance between those two positions is most of what a fast-growing district needs from its representative.

Leigh learned this on the Helena City Council, where planning and funding road projects was a standing item. Infrastructure is a decade-long queue, not an announcement — and the districts that get moved up the queue are the ones with someone in Montgomery who knows the process and keeps asking.

She has worked with local leaders to bring back more than $360,000 in grant funding for community needs and local projects across District 15.

05

Values

Life, Faith and Family

Leigh is a conservative Christian and a member of Church of the Highlands. She and Dennis have been married more than 27 years and raised three children in Shelby County. Protecting unborn life has been one of her stated priorities since her first campaign.

She also believes government overreach is a real thing and that the burden of proof belongs on the state — whether the question is a parent's role in their child's education, a family's medical decisions, or the Second Amendment.

The citizens of District 15 deserve a God-fearing, qualified, conservative, pro-jobs leader fighting for them. Leigh Hulsey

06

Service

Veterans and Military Families

Supporting active-duty service members, military dependents and veterans has been on Leigh's list since she first ran. Alabama asks a great deal of these families. The least the state can do is make sure the paperwork isn't the hardest part of the assignment.

That means licensing that travels, school transfers that don't cost a semester, and a district office where a veteran can reach a human being who will follow up.
