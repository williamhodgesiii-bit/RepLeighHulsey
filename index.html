Meet Leigh

Twelve years at home. A gym in Alabaster. Now a seat in Montgomery.

Leigh grew up in Pelham and graduated from Pelham High School, then Auburn. She spent twelve years at home raising three kids, worked a few years as a paralegal, and then did the thing people talk about but rarely do — she opened her own business. CrossFit Alabaster is still hers.

Small business led to the Alabaster Business Alliance, which led to the Helena City Council, where she worked on municipal budgets, economic development, and the unglamorous business of planning and paying for roads. In 2022, District 15 sent her to the Alabama House.

Priorities

What she's actually working on

Six things District 15 keeps asking about. Tap any card for the detail.

Classrooms That Work

Leigh wrote the FOCUS Act — Alabama's bell-to-bell school phone law. Teachers had been asking for it for years.

HB166 passed the House 79–15 and the Senate 30–2, and Governor Ivey signed it in May 2025. It carves out emergencies, students with an IEP or 504 plan, and genuine instructional use. It also requires school internet safety policies and a social media safety course before eighth grade.

Jobs and Small Business

She has signed the front of a paycheck. That changes how you read a tax bill.

In 2026 Leigh carried HB399, which cuts the maximum tax abatement for data centers from 30 years to 20 unless a project meets real community investment thresholds. Alabama still competes for the jobs — the state just stops writing deals that outlast the people who approved them. It passed the Senate unanimously.

Public Safety

Back the people who run toward the problem, and give them the tools to finish the job.

District 15 is growing fast, and public safety budgets rarely grow at the same speed. Leigh has supported legislation strengthening public safety and works directly with local departments on equipment and grant requests.

Roads and Growth

Everybody wants the growth. Nobody wants the traffic. That's the whole job.

On the Helena City Council, Leigh worked on planning and funding road projects — which is where you learn that infrastructure is a decade-long queue, not a press release. In Montgomery she's pushed to keep District 15 near the front of that queue.

Life, Faith and Family

Protecting unborn life, and protecting the right of families to raise their kids their way.

Leigh is a member of Church of the Highlands. She and her husband Dennis have been married more than 27 years and raised three children in Shelby County. Her conservative record on life and family isn't a position she adopted for a campaign — it's the reason she ran.

Veterans and Military Families

Alabama asks a lot of the families who serve. The paperwork shouldn't be the hard part.

Supporting active-duty service members, military dependents and veterans has been one of Leigh's stated priorities since her first campaign — from licensing and school transfers for military families to making sure veterans in District 15 can reach someone who will actually pick up.
